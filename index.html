The Invitation

In 1976, a double-hulled canoe named Hōkūleʻa (“Star of Gladness”) sailed 2,500 miles from Maui to Tahiti with no compass, no charts, and no instruments, proving that ancestors could have crossed the Pacific on purpose, guided by stars, swells, and birds.

Tonight the family sails in her wake. The Carters’ canoe carries a precious new passenger, and only the whole crew working together can bring the voyage home to Carter Island, where everyone will meet the newest member of the ʻohana.

Tonight, a canoe waits at Carter Island. Its crew? Each of you. Its passenger? Someone very small, and very loved.
